## The game in one paragraph

Boba Shop is a team game where 2 to 4 kids, age 5 and up, run a pretend bubble tea shop for one "day" of about 15 minutes. A tablet plays the shop's boss and its customers. The kids take orders on paper tickets and build every drink by hand from real cups, Play-Doh toppings and small pieces. Everyone wins or loses together.

## Who it is for

- Allen's two kids and the two daughters of a friend family, so four kids at one table.
- Mixed ages, about 5 to 8, so each job needs its own difficulty.
- Allen does not write code. The tablet app will be built by Claude Code from the build instructions.

## Design rules we settled on

- **The kids' hands do the playing.** The tablet runs customers, the clock and the score.
- **Talking is part of the game.** The manager calls out each step, and the makers build from what they hear.
- **The tablet cannot see the cups.** The players check the drinks, so checking is a job.
- **No reading needed.** Orders are spoken and shown as pictures.
- **One team.** Nobody loses to a sibling or friend.
- **No "wrong drink" moment.** The manager catches mistakes and asks for one fix before the drink is served.
- **Remove work for parents.** Almost every piece is bought or found at home, and cleanup is part of the game.

## The four jobs

Each child owns one station. The cashier and the manager handle the order, and the two makers build the drink. The makers never see the ticket, and the manager never touches the cup.

| Job | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Cashier | Plays the tablet. Works out what each customer wants, circles it on a paper ticket, rings up the price, hands the ticket to the manager, and warns the team what the line wants next |
| Manager | Holds the ticket and calls out each step to the makers. Looks at the finished cup, calls any fix, then rings the bell and serves on the tablet. Also starts tea brews, watches supplies, and calls out the time and the goal |
| Topping maker | Makes all three toppings from Play-Doh, keeps the stock bowls full, and scoops what the manager calls into each cup |
| Tea maker | Picks the cup the manager calls, holds it on a filling spot for the topping, adds the tea, ice and sugar, then the lid and straw, and sets the cup on the pickup shelf |

- With 3 players, the jobs are cashier, manager and one maker who does both toppings and tea.
- With 2 players, one child runs the front counter (cashier and manager) and the other makes toppings and tea.
- Jobs rotate every day. A parent can lock a job to a child.
- Anyone with nothing to do may roll pearls for the stock bowl.

## One round

| Phase | Length | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Meet the boss | 1 minute | The boss (the shop owner, on the tablet) asks how many are playing. Each child picks an apron color and gets a job |
| 2. Before opening | 3 minutes | Each job has a picture checklist: make toppings, brew tea, set out cups, stack tickets. No timer. The boss gives a hint about the day |
| 3. Open | 5 minutes | Customers line up and the team serves as many as it can |
| 4. Closing | 3 minutes | The boss inspects three drinks, then stars, badges, decorating and a cleanup song |

## How one order works

1. A customer walks up on the tablet and orders by voice and pictures.
2. The cashier circles the order on the next numbered ticket and rings up the price. The customer pays on screen and moves to a waiting spot. The cashier hands the ticket to the manager.
3. The manager keeps the ticket and calls one step at a time: the cup size, the topping and scoops, then the tea, ice and sugar. The makers answer "Heard!"
4. The tea maker holds the right cup on the filling spot, and the topping maker scoops in the topping. The tea maker then adds the tea, ice and sugar.
5. The manager looks at the cup and compares it to the ticket. If something is off, the manager calls that one fix.
6. The tea maker adds the lid and straw and sets the cup on the pickup shelf. The manager slides the ticket under it, rings the bell, calls the order and taps that customer on the tablet. The customer picks up the drink, reacts and leaves a tip.

In easy mode the ticket travels with the cup and the makers read it themselves. Use it for first games, or when the manager is too young to call the steps.

Two drinks can be in progress at once. Up to three more customers wait in line, each showing a picture of the topping they want.

## Other decisions

- **Cups:** 12 clear cups in two sizes, 6 small and 6 large. Finished drinks stay built until closing. The tablet counts cups, stops ordering a size that has run out, and closes the shop early as "sold out" when all are used.
- **Toppings:** All Play-Doh, one color each, and one topping per order so the colors never mix.

| Topping | Color | How it is made |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boba pearls | Black | Roll a snake, cut it into pieces, roll each piece into a ball |
| Coconut jelly | White | Roll flat, cut into cubes |
| Gummy hearts | Red | Roll flat, press with a heart cutter |

- **Fresh orders:** A stock of each topping is made before opening. Some customers ask for "fresh," which means made on the spot. It costs one coin more.
- **Tea flavors:** Start with three: milk tea (brown), matcha (green) and taro (purple).
- **Tickets:** Numbered 1 to 12, with pictures to circle. The manager holds each ticket on a small stand facing away from the makers, and it ends up under its finished cup on the pickup shelf.
- **Money:** All money is on the tablet. Starting prices: small 3 coins, large 5, fresh adds 1. A fast drink earns a tip of 2 coins and a slower one earns 1.
- **Brewing:** Tea is brewed by the pot. A pot holds 3 servings. When it is empty, the manager adds a leaf token and flips a sand timer, and that flavor waits until the timer runs out.
- **Waiting customers:** They show impatience through body language. On normal difficulty a customer who waits too long in line leaves without buying. On easy, nobody leaves.
- **Closing inspection:** The boss picks three order numbers at random. Each order replays on screen and the team compares it to the cup on that ticket. A correct cup earns a bonus tip.
- **Stars:** The day's money earns 1 to 3 stars against a goal. Stars unlock new toppings and customers.
- **Badges:** The manager earns "perfect day" for three correct inspected cups. The cashier earns "perfect register" for ringing up every total right the first time. The topping maker earns "never ran out." The tea maker's badge is undecided.
- **Decorating the shop:** Money goes into a savings jar that carries over between sessions. One child per day is the decorator and picks one purchase: wallpaper, floor, sign, plants, lights, furniture, a shop pet. Decorations are looks only and never change difficulty. The first version offers colors and patterns, and furniture comes later.

## Difficulty

Orders gain one detail per level, with at most three settings per detail.

| Level | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Tea and one topping |
| 2 | Cup size: small or large |
| 3 | Boba amount: 1, 2 or 3 scoops |
| 4 | Ice: none, some or lots |
| 5 | Sugar: none, half or full |
| 6 | Fresh orders |
| 7 | A longer line |

The cashier has separate levels: picture buttons that add up the price (easy), adding the prices and typing the total (medium), and giving change (hard).

The manager's call has levels too:

| Level | Who knows the order |
| --- | --- |
| Easy | Everyone. The customer orders aloud and the makers may read the ticket |
| Normal | The customer orders aloud, but only the manager holds the ticket and calls the steps |
| Hard | The customer only greets the team aloud. The makers hear the order from the manager alone |

Difficulty is set per job so a 5-year-old and an 8-year-old can share a table.

## What is on the table

Nothing needs a 3D printer.

- **Bought:** 12 clear cups with lids in two sizes that share one lid, wide boba straws, acrylic ice cubes, a desk bell, a sand timer.
- **Play-Doh:** Black, white and red, plus tools the family already owns (roller, plastic knife, small cutters, a scoop, a small heart cutter).
- **Household:** Small lidded containers as tea pots, tokens as tea leaves, white beads as sugar, small bowls with picture labels, a tray as the pickup shelf, a clothespin or card stand as the manager's ticket rail, colored stickers or wristbands as apron badges.
- **Paper:** Order tickets, or laminated cards with a dry-erase marker.
- **Worth printing later:** Station trays with picture labels, a pearl slicer that cuts equal pieces, a ticket rail, a pickup shelf with numbered spots.

## Open questions

1. **How the tea is made and shown in the cup.** This is the biggest open design question. The options so far:
   - Shaker with loose see-through tea pieces (the front-runner): the tea maker loads a shaker with tea pieces, ice and sugar, shakes it and pours it over the toppings. It gives the tea a real making action but looks like colored ice, and it can spill.
   - Stacking layers: thin colored discs, one for a small and two for a large.
   - Lever dispenser: a tower that drops one tea piece per pull.
   - Solid tea piece: one colored block that rests partway down the cup. Judged too easy.
   - Sealed tubes of colored water: look the most real but cannot be poured.
   - Tinted sleeve around the cup: shows the pearls best but has the weakest action.
   - Water beads: ranked last. They are a swallowing hazard for young children, they are wet, and water ruins Play-Doh.
2. Whether one manager can call two drinks at once, or whether it should drop to one.
3. Number of cups (12 to start).
4. Shift length (5 minutes to start).
5. Whether waiting customers leave.
6. Closing inspection of 3 random drinks or every drink.
7. Whether brewing by the pot is fun or a chore.
8. Prices, tips and the coin goals for stars.
9. A badge for the tea maker.
10. Customer art and voices (see below).
11. The boss's name and look.
12. Whether decorating goes in the first build or in version 0.2.

## Art and voice plan

- **During building:** Simple characters drawn in code, as placeholders.
- **Later:** The kids draw customers on paper with a blank face. A parent photographs each one, and the app draws simple eyes and a mouth on top so the customer can blink, talk, smile and frown.
- **Expressions:** At least three per customer: waiting, happy and impatient.
- **Voices:** Recorded family voices for greetings and reactions. Full orders have too many combinations to record, so the tablet's built-in voice speaks them at first.
- **Ingredient pictures:** One matching icon set, the same on the tablet, the tickets and the bowl labels.

## Parked for version 0.2

- Tricksters who underpay or pay with something silly
- Regulars who ask for "the usual"
- Free samples to calm the line
- Changed minds, rush hour and running out of pearls mid-shift
- Riddle orders and orders that disappear after a few seconds
- Silly rules, such as tongs only or no talking
- Delivery orders carried to a parent in another room
- Kid-invented specials
- Froyo as a second menu

## Ideas we tried and dropped

These were considered and rejected, so they do not need to be raised again without a new reason.

| Idea | Why it was dropped |
| --- | --- |
| Only the manager sees the tablet | The other kids could not see the customer react. Everyone now sees the customer, and only the paper ticket is kept from the makers |
| The ticket travels down the line with the cup | The manager had little to do while a drink was made. The manager now calls the steps, and this flow survives as easy mode |
| The manager adds the lid and handles the cup | The manager now directs with words only. The tea maker adds the lid and straw |
| The customer reacts to a wrong drink | Replaced by the manager's check and a fix before serving |
| The cashier rings up at the end of the line | Left the cashier idle. The cashier now takes the order and payment first |
| Real coins and a shared customer purse | Customers are on screen and have no coins to hand over. Money is virtual |
| One solid block as the tea | Too easy, and it reads as a block |
| 8 cups in 3 sizes | Drinks are made quickly, so 12 cups. Two sizes are enough |
| Two toppings in one drink | Play-Doh colors would mix and cleanup would be hard |
| Tea maker scoops the toppings | The topping maker felt cut off. Now the topping maker scoops into the cup the tea maker holds |
| Printing most pieces | Play-Doh tools, acrylic ice and household items already do the job |
| Camera or tag scanning of cups | Fragile. The players check the cups |

## Paper playtest

An adult plays the boss and the customers and reads orders aloud. A phone runs a 5-minute timer. Use real cups, Play-Doh, and any stand-in for tea and ice.

Play one round each way: once with the manager calling the steps, and once in easy mode with the ticket travelling with the cup.

Three things to watch:

- Does every job stay busy, or does someone wait around?
- Is 5 minutes too short, too long or about right?
- Do the kids enjoy the manager's check and the closing inspection?
